Accessibility

In NativeCSS, accessibility isn't an add-on file but a founding decision: native elements, visible focus, respected user preferences. helpers/a11y.css provides the few helper classes - the rest lives inside every component.

Why It's a Core Pillar

A website you can't operate with a keyboard, whose text gets clipped at 200 % zoom, or whose animations trigger dizziness is simply broken for part of its audience. That's not only people with permanent disabilities - it's also someone with a broken arm, bright sunlight on their screen, or a slow connection.

Because NativeCSS builds on native HTML elements (<dialog>, <details>, real radio groups, <input type="range">), many components get keyboard support, focus handling and screen reader roles straight from the browser. Where JavaScript is needed, it follows the WAI-ARIA patterns (combobox, listbox, menu) and stays an enhancement: without the script, the native base functionality still works.

Target: WCAG 2.2 Level AA

Components, tokens and this handbook are measured against the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA. The four principles behind them: perceivable (contrast, text alternatives, captions), operable (keyboard, enough time, no motion traps), understandable (clear labels, error messages) and robust (correct markup that assistive technology can interpret).

A design system can only supply the building blocks. Whether a finished page meets the criteria depends on its content and how it's put together: meaningful alt text, a logical heading structure, descriptive link text, captions for videos and sufficient contrast for your own colors are things no CSS can produce automatically.

Context: Germany's Accessibility Act (BFSG)

Since 28 June 2025, Germany's Barrierefreiheitsstärkungsgesetz (BFSG) has been in force. It transposes the European Accessibility Act (EU Directive 2019/882) and covers many consumer-facing digital products and services - online shops and other e-commerce services, banking services, e-books, and booking and ticketing services in passenger transport, among others. Services provided by micro-enterprises are exempt.

The technical basis is the harmonised European standard EN 301 549, which refers to WCAG for web content. If you aim for WCAG 2.2 AA, you also cover the web criteria referenced there (currently WCAG 2.1 AA). The law additionally requires information on how a service meets the requirements - see Accessibility Statement.

Not legal advice: NativeCSS helps you build accessible websites - but it can't make a site compliant on its own and doesn't replace an audit. Whether and to what extent the BFSG applies to your service is a question for qualified advice.

System-wide Principles

  • Native elements first - <dialog>, <details>, <fieldset>, <progress>/<meter> bring keyboard control, focus management and roles for free, with no ARIA reimplementation.
  • :focus-visible always stays ungated - unlike :hover, which is only active on real pointing devices (see Touch & Mobile Viewports).
  • prefers-reduced-motion is respected globally (the * selector includes html) - no animation has to check it individually (details under User Preferences).
  • Color contrast is part of the token palette: -on-soft text colors per family are checked against their own -100 surface, not guessed (see Colors & Surfaces).
  • .ncss-table-stacked is the one deliberate exception - breaks programmatic table semantics in favor of readability on narrow screens, see Known Limitations.
