Accessibility

In NativeCSS, accessibility is not an add-on but part of the foundation: system settings are respected, focus is always visible, names and target sizes are right. Here you can try it out – with the Inspect button at the bottom right (or Alt+I), clicking a block shows the classes it uses.

Display preferences

<ncss-preferences> lets visitors adapt an accessible page to themselves: colour scheme, contrast, motion, text size, reading font, text spacing, underlined links and a stronger focus ring. Every change applies to the whole page at once and is remembered – try it here.

Not an overlay: It fixes nothing; the page has to be accessible already. System settings stay the default “System” and are never silently overridden.

  • <ncss-preferences>
  • inline
  • settings
  • js/preferences.js
  • js/preferences-early.js
  • components/preferences-fonts.css

Respecting system settings

Less motion, more contrast, less transparency, system colours: under prefers-reduced-motion, reset.css caps all animations globally, and the components handle forced-colors themselves. The checkboxes only simulate this on the sample area.

For your own effects: Write motion as an addition under no-preference – then “no motion” is automatically the stable state.

  • prefers-reduced-motion
  • prefers-contrast
  • prefers-reduced-transparency
  • forced-colors
  • light-dark()

Skip link & hidden text

Reload the page and press Tab once: “Skip to content” appears at the top – .ncss-skip-link, the first element after <body>. .ncss-visually-hidden hides text visually only, screen readers still read it; the -focusable variant shows up on keyboard focus.

Watch out: display:none or hidden hide from screen readers too – for “read out only” always use .ncss-visually-hidden.

  • .ncss-skip-link
  • .ncss-visually-hidden
  • .ncss-visually-hidden-focusable
  • helpers/a11y.css

Focus ring

One consistent ring via :focus-visible: visible for the keyboard, not for mouse and touch – never removed without replacement. As an outline it stays visible in high-contrast mode too. Tab through the elements below.

Tip: In the display preferences above, “Stronger focus outline” switches on a thicker ring with a contrasting halo (data-focus="strong").

  • :focus-visible
  • --ncss-color-focus-ring
  • --ncss-focus-ring-width
  • data-focus="strong"

Accessible names

Every control needs a name that says what it does. Order of preference: visible text, then .ncss-visually-hidden, then aria-label, then aria-labelledby (e.g. a dialog pointing to its heading). The icon itself gets aria-hidden="true".

Watch out: When text is visible, the name must contain it – otherwise voice control (“click Save”) doesn't work.

  • .ncss-visually-hidden
  • aria-label
  • aria-labelledby
  • .ncss-btn--icon
In the handbook: Accessible names →

A screen reader says: “Menu, button” · “Open search, button” · “Copy link, button” – in that order of quality.

Touch: target size & feedback

Click targets are at least 24 × 24 px (WCAG 2.5.8) – .ncss-btn--sm included – and icon buttons are as tall as text buttons. Touch has no hover: feedback comes via :active, and hover effects only live under (hover: hover).

Tip: .ncss-press gives any element the same short press-in as buttons – tap or click the card.

  • .ncss-btn--sm
  • .ncss-btn--icon
  • .ncss-press
  • :active
  • --ncss-motion-duration-fast

Screen only, print only

.ncss-screen-only disappears in print, .ncss-print-only only appears there – for instance the written-out address instead of a map button. Open the print preview (Ctrl+P) to see the swap.

Good to know: The hiding helpers use !important – they beat any component, whatever its layer.

  • .ncss-screen-only
  • .ncss-print-only
  • .ncss-hidden
  • .ncss-invisible
  • helpers/visibility.css
